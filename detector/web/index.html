ABOUT / LIMITS

ALIBI

Automatic Lookout for Irregular Baltic Itineraries. A detector says a ship is behaving unusually. ALIBI checks what the available evidence can explain, and ranks what is left.

Responsible use

  • No live alerts. This console replays fixed, recorded cases.
  • Ranked, not alarmed. The top of the list reads: behaving unusually, and the available evidence does not explain it. It is a list to look at first, not a list of culprits.
  • Unexplained is not evidence of intent. A high value says the sea, the place and the traffic we checked do not account for the behaviour. It does not say why.
  • Missing data lowers confidence. It never raises a score.
  • Identity lists never change the score. EU designations and the GUR listing are shown as context, separately. A GUR listing is not a sanctions designation.
  • Sentences are fixed and written by people. No language model is in the decision path.

Data sources and attribution

  • Generated using E.U. Copernicus Marine Service Information (waves, wind, sea ice, currents).
  • AIS: Fintraffic Digitraffic, licensed CC BY 4.0.
  • AIS history: Danish Maritime Authority AIS archive.
  • EU sanctions list (designated vessels, matched by IMO).
  • OpenSanctions, GUR dataset (Ukrainian Defence Intelligence listing).

Coverage limits

  • Receiver coverage is regional: the Gulf of Finland from our own recording (October 2026) and Danish waters from the DMA archive. Not the whole Baltic.
  • Profiles are young: weeks of data, not years. Ships with few journeys fall back to their class prototype, with lower confidence.
  • Context layers (anchorages, restriction areas) cover Finnish waters only.
  • Correlation with sea state is not proof of cause. Scores are not calibrated probabilities.
  • Early prototype built for the Copernicus Marine Service Hackathon, track Maritime Security.
